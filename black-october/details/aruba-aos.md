**HPE security bulletin HPESBNW05162 rev.1 — HPE CVSS 3.1: eight CVEs at 10.0 Critical (CVE-2026-79844 through CVE-2026-79851); 33 of the 72 CVEs are Critical (9.0 or higher), 31 High, 7 Medium and 1 Low; HPE bulletin severity: Critical. NVD lists HPE's scores as secondary and has not published its own (status "Awaiting Analysis" on 2026-10-26). Not in CISA KEV.** The bulletin covers the HPE Networking (formerly Aruba) wireless operating systems **AOS-8** and **AOS-10** on Mobility Conductors, Mobility Controllers, Mobility Gateways managed by HPE Networking Central, AOS-10 access points (AOS-AP) and AOS-8 Instant access points (AOS-IAP), including virtual appliances. HPE says any hardware or VM compatible with an affected version is vulnerable, and that its model list is illustrative only. The worst issues let an **unauthenticated network attacker** run commands as a privileged user on the device through the web-based management interface, the CLI/SSH daemon, or **PAPI** (Aruba's access point management protocol, UDP 8211). A second large group needs only **adjacent-network** (same layer-2 segment or radio) access. Worst case: full takeover of the controller, gateway or AP, which carries client traffic and holds network and authentication configuration. Some flaws are qualified by HPE: CVE-2026-79844, CVE-2026-79846, CVE-2026-79891 and CVE-2026-95711 need "certain preconditions outside of the attacker's control", which HPE does not describe.

**CVE ID conflict:** HPE's bulletin, CSAF file and BleepingComputer's coverage list **CVE-2026-79845** as an AOS-10 PAPI authentication bypass (HPE CVSS 10.0). On 2026-10-26 the CVE.org record and NVD entry for that ID belong to a **different, unrelated** VulDB-assigned issue (an SQL injection in code-projects Simple Inventory System, published 2026-08-25). HPE published CVE records for the other 71 IDs only. The CVE.org link above is generated as required and is **not** evidence of an HPE record. Track the AOS-10 PAPI flaw by HPE's bulletin, not by that CVE record, until HPE or CVE.org corrects it.

**CISA KEV:** Not listed (catalog 2026.10.23, checked 2026-10-26). HPE says it is not aware of public discussion or exploit code as of 2026-10-22, CISA's ADP SSVC entries record "Exploitation: none", and no exploitation reports were found through 2026-10-26. ## Detection

### Inventory and versions

1. List every AOS-8 Mobility Conductor, Mobility Controller (hardware and Virtual Mobility Controller), AOS-8 Instant AP, AOS-10 gateway and AOS-10 AP, including standby/cluster members and spares that may be powered on later. For Central-managed AOS-10 devices, use Central's device and firmware inventory. On AOS-8 controllers and conductors, the read-only CLI command `show version` reports the running AOS version. It changes no state. It was not exercised on a real product for this guide.
2. Compare each running version with HPE's table:

   | Branch | Affected (HPE bulletin) | Fixed |
   |---|---|---|
   | AOS-10.8 | 10.8.1.1 and below | 10.8.1.2 or later |
   | AOS-10.7 | 10.7.2.6 and below | 10.7.2.7 or later |
   | AOS-10.4 | 10.4.1.13 and below | 10.4.1.14 or later |
   | AOS-8.13 | 8.13.3.0 and below | 8.13.3.1 or later |
   | AOS-8.10 | 8.10.0.23 and below | 8.10.0.24 or later |

   The HPE CNA records encode the same ranges starting at x.y.0.0 (for example 8.13.0.0 through 8.13.3.0). **Other branches are not cleared:** HPE says versions past End of Maintenance are presumed affected and not covered, and that it has not assessed versions past End of Support. Treat any other AOS-8 or AOS-10 branch as **affected with no fix in that branch**.
3. Record which family each device runs. Per the HPE CSAF file and CNA records, 38 CVEs affect AOS-8 only, 27 affect AOS-10 only (counting CVE-2026-79845 from the CSAF file) and 7 affect both (CVE-2026-79852, CVE-2026-79862, CVE-2026-79863, CVE-2026-79872, CVE-2026-95704, CVE-2026-95710, CVE-2026-95717). Both families need the update; the per-family split only matters for prioritizing which interfaces to restrict first.

### Exposure and input reachability

1. **Management plane:** find every address on which each device accepts SSH, the web-based management interface (WebUI/HTTPS) and the REST API. Check from outside your perimeter and from user, guest and IoT VLANs, not only from the management network. HPE's workaround assumes these are confined to a dedicated management VLAN or firewall-restricted; any device where they are reachable from user or guest segments or the internet is **exposed**.
2. **PAPI (UDP 8211):** many of the Critical flaws arrive over PAPI. Identify which networks can send UDP 8211 to controllers, conductors, gateways and APs. Controller-AP and cluster traffic needs it, so it may cross VLANs and WAN links you consider internal.
3. **Adjacent attack surface:** 14 Critical and 9 High CVEs need only adjacent-network access. Any user, guest or IoT segment that shares a layer-2 domain with a device's interface, and the wireless side for CVE-2026-79876, counts as reachable. A perimeter firewall does not mitigate this group.
4. If you cannot determine reachability for a device, record it as **exposed**, not safe.

### Logs and indicators

HPE published **no indicators of compromise** and no detection signatures for HPESBNW05162. No vendor or CISA hunting guidance was found as of 2026-10-26. Useful checks:

- Review device audit/accounting logs (local, syslog and RADIUS/TACACS+ accounting, which HPE's workaround recommends enabling) for administrator logins from unexpected sources, logins without a matching AAA record, new local admin users, and configuration changes nobody made.
- Look for unexpected reboots, process crashes or datapath restarts; many of these bugs are buffer overflows, and failed attempts may crash services.
- In firewall and NetFlow data, look for UDP 8211 from hosts that are not your APs or controllers, and for SSH or HTTPS management sessions from user or guest networks.

These checks are weak. AOS devices give limited visibility into the underlying OS, attackers with root can alter local logs, and adjacent attacks may never cross a monitored firewall. A clean result does **not** prove a device was not compromised. No companion script was created for this item: version inventory comes from the device CLI or Central, and no reliable offline indicator exists to script against.

## Remediation

1. **Upgrade** every device to **AOS-10.8.1.2+, 10.7.2.7+, 10.4.1.14+, AOS-8.13.3.1+ or 8.10.0.24+**, downloading firmware only from the [HPE Networking Support Portal](https://networkingsupport.hpe.com). For Central-managed AOS-10 gateways and APs, schedule the upgrade through Central. Devices on other branches must move to a supported fixed branch; HPE does not cover them.
2. Plan for reboots and coverage loss. Upgrade conductors, controllers or gateways and their APs in the order HPE's release notes and upgrade guides require, keep cluster and AP firmware compatible, and back up configurations first. Read the release notes for each target version before upgrading; this guide did not review them.
3. **Until upgraded**, apply HPE's workaround: restrict the CLI and web-based management interfaces to a dedicated layer-2 segment/VLAN and/or firewall policy at layer 3 and above, with accounting enabled to track user activity. This changes device or firewall configuration, so test it so you do not lock yourself out.
4. Additional containment (not from HPE): permit UDP 8211 only between your own APs, controllers and gateways, and keep user, guest and IoT segments out of the layer-2 domains of device management interfaces. Neither step addresses the Wi-Fi frame-processing flaw or adjacent attackers who are already inside an allowed segment; only the upgrade does.
5. Patching prevents new exploitation. It does not remove accounts, configuration changes or persistence an attacker already added. Preserve logs and a configuration backup before upgrading a device you suspect.

## Verification

- Confirm the running version on every device and cluster member (`show version` on AOS-8 controllers and conductors, or Central's firmware view for AOS-10) is at or above the fixed build for its branch. Confirm APs actually upgraded, not just the controller or the Central target setting.
- Re-test exposure from user, guest and external vantage points: SSH, WebUI/API and UDP 8211 should be unreachable except from the intended management and infrastructure networks.
- Confirm normal operation: client association, AP connectivity to controllers/gateways, and admin login through your AAA server with accounting records appearing.
- Do not send exploit payloads, malformed PAPI packets or authentication-bypass attempts to production devices to "test" the fix.

## Compromise response

- If you find unexplained admin sessions, configuration changes or crashes on a device that ran an affected version with exposed interfaces, preserve logs, `show` output and a configuration backup, then contact HPE support. Root-level code execution can change the device in ways configuration review will not show.
- Reinstall trusted firmware from the HPE portal and restore a reviewed configuration, or replace the device, if its integrity cannot be established.
- Rotate secrets the device holds or could intercept: local admin passwords, RADIUS/TACACS+ shared secrets, pre-shared Wi-Fi keys, certificates and private keys used for captive portal or 802.1X, SNMP communities, and Central/API credentials.
- Hunt downstream: a compromised controller or gateway sees client traffic and talks to directory and authentication servers. Review those for logins and traffic from the device's addresses.
